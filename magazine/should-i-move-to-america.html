Should I Move to America? Big Sister Advice Before You Pack

The dream is real. So is the paperwork, the cost and the distance. Here's the honest version from someone who calls America home.

By Tashia Monique  ·  7 min read  ·  23 November 2026

I get this question all the time, usually right after someone hears my accent. "Should I move to America?" And honestly, I love that you're dreaming big. America is home to me. I love her, and I know her. So let me give you the real, the way I'd tell my own little sister.

The Advice

Get clear on what you're moving toward

"I just need to get out of here" is a feeling, not a plan. What do you want your life to look like? A bigger career? A fresh start? Warm weather? More room to be yourself? Write it down. Then ask if America is the only place that gives you that.

The good stuff is real

There's big energy in the US. People talk about their goals out loud. Starting something new is normal, and in many cities you'll find people from everywhere building something. If you want room to grow and you're ready to hustle, it can feel like home fast.

Pick your city like you pick a partner

America is not one place. New York, Atlanta, Houston and Los Angeles are different worlds. Look at cost of living, weather, work in your field, and whether you'll find your people there. Visit first if you can.

Love the dream. Then love yourself enough to plan for the hard parts.

The Real Talk

The visa is the first door

Most people can't just move. You usually need a work visa, a family tie, or an employer who sponsors you. Start with the official US government visa information, and talk to an immigration lawyer before you quit your job or sell anything.

It can cost more than you think

Rent in big cities is high. Health care is often tied to your job, and it isn't free at the point of use the way the NHS is. Paid time off can be a lot less than you're used to. Build that into your numbers.

Distance is heavy

Missing birthdays, weddings and Sunday dinners back home hits harder than people expect. Time zones make it worse. Plan how you'll stay close.

Race shows up differently

If you're Black, America will see you through its own history. There is deep community and pride, and there are real challenges too. Talk to Black people who live where you want to go. Ask them for the truth.

The Game Plan
  1. Name your why.One sentence on what this move gives you.
  2. Find your visa route first.Before you fall in love with an apartment.
  3. Do a money test.Price out rent, health cover and a six-month cushion in your chosen city.
  4. Visit like a local.Go to a grocery store, ride the bus, sit in traffic at 5 p.m.
  5. Time your leap.Big moves go better in a season that supports change. Know yours before you sign anything.

Whether you go or stay, the point is the same. Build a life that feels like yours, on purpose.
